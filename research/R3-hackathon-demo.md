1. Hackathon rules, judging, and requirements (from official pages)
Submission Period ends Friday, October 30, 2026 at 10:00 am Pacific Time. Judging runs December 1–15, 2026; winners announced on or around January 11, 2027. https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules (accessed Oct 2026).
Stage One is a pass/fail viability check: “whether the ideas meet a baseline level of viability, in that the Project reasonably fits the theme and reasonably applies the required APIs/SDKs… A Project reasonably fits the theme if it is a genuine attempt at the track's stated goal, not a superficial rebrand of an unrelated idea.” Stage Two scores 1–5 on four equally weighted criteria: Technological Implementation (“How well is the project built, and how effectively does it use Nebius Token Factory or AI Cloud model(s), and NVIDIA Nemotron or other NVIDIA open source models as part of the solution?”), Design (“Does the project deliver a complete, coherent product experience not just a technical proof of concept?”), Potential Impact (“Does the project make a credible, specific case for solving a real problem for a real audience and does the solution actually address it based on what's demonstrated?”), and Quality of the Idea (“Is this a creative, non-obvious use of Nebius Token Factory or AI Cloud model(s), and NVIDIA Nemotron or other NVIDIA open source models and does the team show genuine understanding of the problem space?”). https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules; https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/updates/46204-here-s-how-judging-works (~1 month ago).
Coding and Agentic Engineering tip from organizers: “a code-completion wrapper is a start; an agent that plans, writes, tests, and iterates on a real repo with minimal human input is where it gets interesting.” https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/updates/46204-here-s-how-judging-works.
Requirements include a working demo URL (hosted app or test build), a public YouTube video ≤3 minutes that shows the project working and how Nebius Token Factory and NVIDIA Nemotron (or other NVIDIA open models) were used, a public repo (GitHub/GitLab/Bitbucket) with an open-source license (Apache 2.0, MIT, or MPL 2.0) visible at the top, a README with setup instructions that highlights Nemotron/Token Factory usage, and feedback on the tools. Judges “are not required to test the Project and may choose to judge based solely on the text description, images, and video.” All materials must be in English or accompanied by English translations. https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules; https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/ (main page).
Feedback is required and separately prize-eligible (Most Valuable Feedback: $100 + NVIDIA swag for 10 winners). Organizers advise: be specific about what you used Token Factory/AI Cloud/Nemotron for, what worked, what needs work, onboarding experience, and whether you’d build with them again. “Treat your video like a pitch, not a tutorial… Lead with the problem, show the solution working… Name Nebius Token Factory/AI Cloud and your NVIDIA model… and call them out clearly — with audio — in your demo video.” https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/updates/46205-how-to-build-a-winning-project (~20 days ago); https://nebiusglobalaihackathon.devpost.com/updates (prizes update ~6 days ago).

4. Opinion: demo story options for a PR reviewer with the stated benchmark result
Opinion: The honest middle-of-pack result (better than Copilot, below the leaders, ~3¢/PR on an open model) is an asset if framed around cost, openness, and measurable filtering rather than “we beat everyone.” Judges can score solely on the video/description, so the 3-minute pitch must carry the four criteria.
Option A (Impact + Technological Implementation focus): Lead with the real cost of noisy AI reviews (developers ignore or disable bots). Show a live PR where TraceHound’s dual-reviewer + verifier keeps a real defect and drops a false positive, then overlay the benchmark numbers and the ~3¢ cost. Explicitly name Nemotron on Token Factory, show the keep/drop prompt and evidence quote, and end with a one-line “this is what open models can do when you filter hard.” This scores Technological Implementation via clear Nemotron/Token Factory usage and Potential Impact via a specific, demonstrated audience problem.
Option B (Quality of Idea + Design focus): Frame the non-obvious insight as “a 1–5 confidence score failed to separate true from false findings, so we built an evidence-gated keep/drop verifier that must quote code or a failing input.” Demo the full product surface (GitHub comment, linked evidence, one-click dismiss) on a real small PR, then briefly show the public 50-PR comparison table. This makes Design feel like a coherent product and Quality of the Idea feel like genuine problem-space understanding rather than another wrapper.
Option C (Balanced, track-aligned agent story): Show an agentic loop—Nemotron generates candidates, a second pass adds repo context, the verifier decides keep/drop with quoted evidence, and a simple static check or test run filters further—running on a small public repo with minimal human input. Cut to the cost and the “better than Copilot, honest mid-pack” slide. This matches the organizer tip about agents that plan/write/test/iterate and gives equal weight to all four criteria without overclaiming.
